Edge computing refers to the practice of processing data closer to where it is generated, 
rather than sending it to a centralised cloud server. This approach reduces the amount of 
data that must be transmitted over a network, which in turn lowers latency and decreases 
bandwidth costs. In the context of artificial intelligence, edge computing enables machine 
learning models to run directly on local devices such as smartphones, laptops, or IoT sensors.

The key advantage of edge AI is privacy: sensitive data such as medical records, financial 
transactions, or personal communications can be processed locally without ever leaving the 
device. This is particularly important in regulated industries where data residency and 
compliance requirements restrict the use of external cloud services.

However, edge AI also introduces challenges. Local devices have limited computational 
resources compared to cloud servers, meaning that only smaller, more efficient models 
can be deployed at the edge. Balancing model performance with resource constraints 
remains an active area of research in the field.
